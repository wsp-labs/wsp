# Moving between threads and the palette

Three ways move you between threads with the keyboard. `Ctrl+Tab` steps down the sidebar. The palette searches threads and actions. The jump keys open a row of the palette's Tasks group by its number.

In the chords below, `mod` is `Cmd` on a Mac and `Ctrl` elsewhere. You can change every chord in Settings > Keybindings. The [keyboard shortcuts](/features/shortcuts) page lists them all.

## Step through the sidebar

Press `Ctrl+Tab` to open the next thread down the sidebar, and `Ctrl+Shift+Tab` to open the one above. The thread opens at once, with the cursor in its composer, and its row scrolls into view. Hold the keys to keep stepping. After the last thread it starts again at the top.

The step stays at the level of the thread you have open:

- On a top-level thread, it goes through the top-level threads and skips sub-threads.
- On a top-level thread in **Settled**, it goes through the settled top-level threads.
- On a sub-thread, it goes through the other threads started by the same thread. A thread that also shows in **Needs you** steps by its place in its tree.

The step skips the threads of a folded section. To open a thread at another level, click its row or use the palette.

`mod+alt+left` and `mod+alt+right` do the same step. `mod+alt+up` and `mod+alt+down` step through the threads of the open project only.

When the right panel has more than one tab and holds focus, `Ctrl+Tab` steps through the panel's tabs instead. A focused terminal keeps `Ctrl+Tab` for itself.

## The palette

Press `mod+k` or `mod+shift+p`, or click **Search** at the top of the sidebar, to open the palette.

<Image src="/shots/docs-palette.webp" alt="The palette with the word settle typed." />

Before you type, the palette shows three groups:

- **Actions**: things to do, such as **New thread**, **Add a project**, **Settings** and **Report a bug**, and the actions of the open project.
- **Tasks**: one row for each project folder and each worktree that has a thread. Each row shows its jump key.
- **Recent threads**: the 12 threads started last.

When you type, it searches the actions, the Tasks rows, the titles of your threads, and the words in their messages. A match in a message shows under **In messages**, with the words around it.

To search the actions only, start with `>`.

An action that cannot run now shows grey, with the reason in place of its description.

The palette does not search files. To find a file, press `mod+p`. See [Files](/features/files).

## Jump to a row

`mod+1` to `mod+9` open the rows of the palette's **Tasks** group by their number. `mod+1` is the first row. The rows are in the order in which wsp made them, oldest first, and the order does not change while you work. A project folder or a worktree with no thread has no row, and each worktree that has a thread has a row of its own. If there is no row at that number, nothing happens.

The sidebar shows threads, not these rows, so the numbers do not match the sidebar. Open the palette to see which row has which number.

## Go to the next thread that needs you

Press `mod+alt+u` to open the next thread that waits for you. A thread waits for you when it asks a question or a permission. It also waits for you when it finished or failed, and no window has shown that yet. It goes down the sidebar from the open thread and starts again at the top. If no thread waits for you, nothing happens.

## In a browser tab

A browser keeps some chords for itself: `Ctrl+Tab`, `mod` with a number, `mod+t`, `mod+n`, and on a Mac `Cmd+Option` with the arrows. In a browser tab, use the palette or the sidebar instead, for a new thread too.
